# 218 · Race for Congress: Project Plan

**One line:** a nonpartisan, interactive simulator for the 2026 US midterms. *Move the national vote, watch control of Congress change hands.* Think of the BBC "swingometer", made interactive, mobile and shareable.

- **Election Day:** Tue Nov 3, 2026
- **Target public launch:** Tue Oct 20, 2026
- **Repo:** https://github.com/EasyLearnJava/race-for-congress
- **Hosting:** Vercel (`race-for-congress.vercel.app`)

### Why the timing matters
Nine states vote on **new House maps** this year: AL, CA, FL, LA, NC, OH, TN, TX and UT. Missouri's new map was blocked. AP estimates the new lines could net Republicans up to nine seats. That has two consequences:
1. The last House results are the wrong starting point for those districts. We measure each district's lean on the **2026 lines**.
2. "How far did redistricting move the finish line?" is our most newsworthy feature.

---

## 1. Users and their jobs

1. **Play (30 sec):** what happens if the country leans a bit further one way?
2. **Personal (60 sec):** how competitive is *my* district, and what decides it?
3. **Predict and share (3 min):** make my own call and post it.

## 2. Features, in priority order

### Must have: the minimum demo (Day 3)
- **Seat bar:** 435 seats sorted Democratic to Republican, a bold 218 line and live counts.
- **National vote slider:** runs from R+10 to D+10.
  - Markers: today's polling average, the 2010 red wave (R+6.8), the 2018 blue wave (D+8.6).
  - Presets: *Polls today*, *2018-style wave*, *Dead even*.
- **Equal-area hex map:** one hexagon per district, so big rural districts don't drown out dense cities. Recolors live.
- **"Flips at" number for every seat:** "This seat flips at D+1.4."
- **Tipping-point list:** the 10 seats closest to the 218 line at the current setting.
- **Race panel:** opens when you tap a seat.
  - Shows lean, flip point, candidates, incumbent, expert rating, a "new lines" badge and past results where comparable.
  - Bottom sheet on mobile, side drawer on desktop.
- **Plain-English headline:** generated by code, not AI: "At {vote}, {party} wins {n} seats, a majority by {k}."
- **Every state is a link:** the URL holds the current setting, so the back button and sharing work.
- **Method and sources page:** the math in plain English, the data sources, the limitations, "simulation, not a forecast", and a vote.gov link.

### Should have: the standouts (Days 4–7)
- **Make your call:**
  - a stack of cards for the 30–50 competitive seats
  - **D / Toss-up / R** buttons (not left/right swipes)
  - ends with your seat count and an image to share.
- **Share images:** a link preview (1200×630) and a phone-story image (9:16): "My 2026 call: D {n} – R {m}".
- **Redistricting effect:** a toggle between 2024 and 2026 maps, plus the **finish line**: "Democrats need to win the national vote by X to reach 218 (was Y on the old maps)."
- **Run 10,000 elections:** a simulation in a background thread; the results fill a histogram; "Majority in {p}% of simulations under your assumptions."
- **Follow the money:**
  - fundraising and outside spending per seat, from the FEC
  - in the final 20 days (from Oct 14), outside spending over $1,000 must be reported within 24h, so there's fresh data every day.
- **AI race summaries:** see §6.
- **Senate mode ("51"):**
  - 35 races: 33 regular plus specials in Ohio and Florida
  - state tile map and a 50/51 line; the Vice President breaks ties, so Democrats need a net gain of 4
  - a candidate-strength adjustment per race.
- **Find my district:** address or location, matched to the 2026 lines (see §3).
- **Bring your own CSV:** upload your own ratings and the app redraws.

### Could have: election week and extras
- **Election Night companion:**
  - countdown to poll closings, and "what to watch at 7:00 / 7:30 / 8:00 ET"
  - mark races as called; "needs X of N uncalled seats"
  - TV mode
  - experimental: an estimate of the national vote from the called seats.
- **"How did your call do?" scorecard** after the results. Counting goes on for days in California and the ranked-choice states.
- **Ask 218:** a chat that answers only by looking up the app's data.
- **Pulse:** most-viewed races and the crowd's calls, labeled unscientific.
- **Backtest:** "Given the actual 2024 national vote, the model called N of 435 seats correctly."
- **Embeddable widget** for local newsletters.
- **Governors** (36 races).

### Won't build
Logins, comments, poll scraping, a live AP feed, endorsements, or anything that stores a person's political views.

## 3. Data (CSV first)

**Flow:** raw downloads (`data/raw/`, not committed) → Python (pandas + pandera checks) → **`data/clean/*.csv` committed as the source of truth** → JSON when the site builds.

| CSV | Key columns | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `races_house_2026.csv` | id (PA-07), state, `new_lines`, `pres24_margin` (2026 lines), `pres24_margin_old_lines`, incumbent, `inc_running`, nominees, `special_case`, expert rating | Dave's Redistricting App or The Downballot (presidential results by district); state election offices, Ballotpedia or Wikipedia (nominees); expert ratings with credit |
| `races_senate_2026.csv` | state, special, incumbent, nominees, `pres24_margin` | same |
| `house_history.csv` | district, year, Dem/Rep votes | MIT Election Data + Science Lab (1976–2024) |
| `fec_candidates.csv` | candidate, race, raised, cash on hand, as-of date | OpenFEC API |
| `outside_spending.csv` | race, for/against each party, last 24h | OpenFEC Schedule E |
| `hex_layout.csv` | id, q, r | a published House hex layout (check its license), with the 9 redrawn states hand-fixed |
| `poll_closings.csv` | state, closing time (ET) | official state sources |
| config | polling average, as-of date, source | updated daily by hand (538 shut down in 2025, so cite the average used) |

**Rules**
- Measure lean with **presidential results by district**, not the last House result, which is distorted by uncontested races and incumbents.
- **Validation:** 435 rows, district counts per state match each state's seat count, every race has hex coordinates, every FEC candidate matches a race (fuzzy match plus a manual `overrides.csv`).
- **Special cases override the model:** uncontested seats, same-party finalists in CA/WA, strong independents, ranked-choice states (AK/ME), possible Georgia runoffs.
- **Find my district:**
  - Not by ZIP code, because ZIP codes cross district lines.
  - The Census address tool may still return the old districts.
  - Instead, turn the address or location into coordinates and check which 2026 district boundary contains the point, **in the browser**. Boundaries are simplified with mapshaper and loaded only when needed. Nothing goes to a server.
- **FEC rule:** individual donor names may not be used commercially or for fundraising. Use candidate and committee totals only.
- **Fresh data:** a nightly GitHub Action fetches FEC data, validates it, commits if anything changed, and Vercel redeploys. The git history doubles as daily snapshots.

## 4. Model (simple and easy to explain)

- `lean = district 2024 presidential margin − national margin (≈ R+1.5)`
- `margin = lean + national vote + incumbent bonus` (±2–3 points if the incumbent runs, 0 for an open seat)
- **Basic version:** the winner is whoever's margin is positive. **Flip point** = −(lean + incumbent bonus).
- **Simulation:** each run adds one **shared national error** (about 3 points; polls miss in the same direction everywhere) plus a district error (about 4–5 points). 10,000 runs give a range of seat counts and the chance of reaching 218. A fixed random seed means shared links reproduce the same result.
- **Finish line:** the national vote at which expected seats = 218, on the old and new maps. The difference is the redistricting effect.
- Expert ratings are shown **next to** the model, never mixed into it. The error sizes are tunable and calibrated by the backtest.
- Lives in `src/lib/model/` as plain functions with unit tests.

## 5. UX and UI

**Principles**
1. Wow within 10 seconds: the seat bar animates in and the slider invites a drag.
2. One main action per screen; advanced controls hidden behind "Advanced".
3. Start at today's polling average, not zero.
4. Everything is a link, and everything can be undone.
5. **Neutral by design:**
   - both parties' colors at the same brightness
   - the same template for every race, no loaded words
   - toss-ups in neutral sand, not purple
   - Democrats always on the left in blue, Republicans on the right in red.
6. Never rely on color alone: labels like "Lean R" and a "view as table" option.
7. A 4-card "New to US politics?" guide for a global audience.

```
Desktop
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 218   House · Senate · Money · Election Night · Method   ⌘K   23 days │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ "At D+x.x, Democrats win ### seats — a majority by #."                │
│ DEM ###  ████████████████▒▒▒▒│▒▒▒▒████████████████  REP ###           │
│                             218                                       │
│ R+10 ────────────────●───────────────── D+10   [Polls today] [Reset]  │
├────────────────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────┤

│ [Make your call]  [Run 10,000 elections]   │ [2024 maps ⇄ 2026 maps]  │

Mobile: the seat bar and slider stay pinned while scrolling; the main
```

**Interaction details**
- Arrow keys move the slider ±0.5; screen readers hear the value in words.
- Flipped seats pulse once and counters roll; animation is off for users who prefer reduced motion.
- "What changed" line: "vs. polls today: +7 D: XX-07, XX-02…"
- ⌘K search across districts, states and candidates.
- Every race panel ends with next steps: make your call · compare · share · official voting info.
- Type: Newsreader (serif headlines) and Inter (interface, fixed-width numbers).
- Themes: off-white "paper" light theme and a real dark mode.
- A freshness label on every section, e.g. "FEC data as of Oct 18".

## 6. AI: grounded, cheap and safe

**Rule: code for numbers, AI for sentences.**

1. **Race summaries, generated in advance**
   - Each race's data row (and nothing else) goes to `claude-opus-5-5` through the **Message Batches API** (asynchronous, 50% cheaper), with JSON via `output_config.format`. Well under $10 for about 470 races.
   - Checks:
     - every number in a summary must appear in its data row
     - **party-swap test:** swap the party labels and the tone must flip symmetrically
     - a banned-words list, plus a second AI pass that grades neutrality
   - A person reviews every Lean and Toss-up race. Each summary is labeled "AI summary from FEC and election data".
2. **Ask 218 chat (extra)**
   - Strict lookup tools: find races, get a race, simulate, compare. It answers only from tool results.
   - It never says who to vote for; voting logistics get a vote.gov link.
   - Settings: `tool_choice: auto` (forced tool choice returns a 400 on this model), effort `low`, cached system prompt and tools, streamed replies, handle `refusal` stop reasons.
   - Rate limit per IP, a daily spending cap, and an off switch.
   - A few cents per question; `claude-haiku-5-5` costs about 1/40th as much if traffic explodes (owner's call).

## 7. Analytics

**For us:** Vercel Web Analytics and Speed Insights; PostHog for events and funnels.

| Event | Details recorded |
|---|---|
| `vote_slider_changed` | value range, how (drag / keys / preset) |
| `race_opened` | race id, where from (map / list / search) |
| `sim_run`, `maps_toggled` | — |
| `call_progress` | number of seats called, finished or not |
| `share_clicked` | link / image / story / embed |
| `shared_visit` | arrived through a shared link |
| `find_district_used` | address or location, success |
| `ai_summary_viewed`, `ai_question` | question category |
| `embed_loaded` | host site |

**What to watch:**
- share of visitors who move the slider or open a seat within 30 seconds
- how many finish Make your call
- share rate, and visits from shared links divided by shares
- visitors who return on election night
- mobile load under 2 seconds

**Privacy:** someone's map is a political opinion. Record counts only, never an individual's party picks; crowd totals use a separate anonymous counter; no screen recording on call screens; no login.

**For users:** rule-based insight cards (biggest cash gap among toss-ups, crossover seats, model vs. experts, most outside money in 24h), Pulse, a "your call vs. model vs. experts vs. crowd" comparison, and a public counter of calls made.

## 11. Three-minute demo script
1. **Hook:** "435 seats, 218 to win, and 9 states redrew their maps this year."
2. Drag from polls-today to D+5; seats flip; point to the tipping-point seat.
3. Flip the map toggle: "the finish line moved by X points."
4. Run 10,000 elections.
5. A judge's address: their district, flip point, money and AI summary.
6. QR code: judges make calls on their phones and the live counter ticks up.
7. Real visitor numbers since launch.
8. **Close:** "CSV in, insight out. Built with coding agents. Neutral by design."

## 12. Risks

| Risk | How to handle it |
|---|---|
| Wrong data (names, new lines) | validation script, manual review of the ~80 most competitive seats, a "Report an error" button |
| Looking biased | matching design and templates, a public method page, neutral-wording review, party-swap test |
| AI making things up | summaries written in advance from data only, checked and labeled; chat answers only through lookups |
| Traffic spike or AI bill | static pages, rate limits, spending cap, off switch |
| Building too much | the Day 3 minimum demo as the cut line; stick to the won't-build list |
